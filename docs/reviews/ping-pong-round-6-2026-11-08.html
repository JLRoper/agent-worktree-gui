The speed bumps

  1. A pipeline row takes two writes. add always creates the row queued; a second pipeline POST puts it on a stage. This run's two posts are 22:49:15.904Z and 22:49:24.164Z, 8.3 s apart. A crash between them leaves a queued row with no stage, which reads like ordinary work and is easy to miss.
  2. The artifact path is shared across rounds, so the tree starts holding the last round's work. The tree was cut from 985a84b and already held draft.md (4349 bytes) and critique.md (12284 bytes) from round 5. The drafter's commit is 9 insertions, 28 deletions: it overwrote the old draft. Round 2 will overwrite this one. The only per-round identity is the stage run's pin (commit + blob + sha256), never the path.

The bundle's ping-pong.gates[0] carries decision: null. gateAfter only answers a gate that declares a decision (lib/task-pipeline-drive.js:722-726), so the loop edge H-02 is taken in kind order. If the critic says accept, the driver still enters draft visit 2; if it says revise, the driver cannot route the rework. The next move is the cap, not the verdict.

The wait is the panel's job card plus the sweep, not the worker. For a nine-minute stage that is tolerable; for a one-minute stage it is most of the round.
