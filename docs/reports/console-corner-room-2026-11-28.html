The console corner: which number stopped the drag, and the one band that replaces it

Measured and fixed on 2026-11-28 in agent-worktree-gui, row #411. Branch wtgui/row-411-is-the-complete-brief-the-turn-8c3221, cut from the turnover branch 20261127_2134_TASK-405_row-405-is-the-complete and rebased onto origin/main's 1.194.3 for its landing. Version 1.195.0.

The short answer. The drag was stopped by the room number, and that room was computed from window.innerHeight, the viewport's height in visual pixels, against a top edge in layout pixels. The transcript's scroll room was not the stopper: its content never grew while the console grew, so it never bounded the drag. On a 90% page the room was short and the box stopped well inside the pane; on a 110% page it was too tall and the box drew past the bottom. The pane's own bottom was never consulted at all. The fix is one band from one module: the stop is min(pane room, viewport room), in one unit, and the record names which edge it was. The grip's 406 lines left public/app.js for public/core/console-grip.js, and four console gates now read that band instead of re-deriving it.

1. What the reader reported, and what was measured

Dragging the console box's bottom-right corner upward stops it well before the available room, on every zoom and window height tried. The console is the turn-output box: a pre.job-out inside .out-box, with a corner grip in the bottom-right, inside a turn inside the pane's scrollport (.job-body).

The old grip carried four numbers that could stop it: the floor (a minimum height), the room (how far the bottom may travel), the cap (the CSS max-height), and the top (the frozen top edge the height is measured from). A fifth candidate sits behind the box: the transcript's scroll room, which decides whether growing the box scrolls the turn.

The four numbers, one by one

  • Floor - not the stopper in any measured case: the stop height (405.89 to 695.19) is far above the floor (min-height: 34 for a grip at 100%, 37.4 at 110%).
  • Room - the stopper in all six cases: the stop height equals the recorded room to the pixel.
  • Cap - not reached: the cap is 250 (32vh of 900) to 230, and every stop is above 405.
  • Top - not the stopper: the recorded top and the drawn box's top agree within 0.5px from press to release, so the frozen top did not drift and did not eat the travel.
  • Transcript scroll room - not the stopper, and this is the prime suspect cleared: while the box grew, the transcript's scrollHeight - clientHeight did not fall to the travel that was being refused. The scrollport's own height was not the value the drag compared against; the room was.

3. Why the room was wrong: visual pixels against layout pixels

The old room was

room = Math.max(cap, innerHeight - top / zoom - 8)        // public/app.js:10829 (base)

innerHeight is the viewport height in visual pixels and does not change with the page's zoom. top is a getBoundingClientRect().top, also visual, so dividing it by the zoom turns it into a layout length; the two terms are then in different units. The box's height is set in layout pixels. Written in one unit, the viewport room is innerHeight / zoom - top - 8. The old expression is short by innerHeight * (1 - 1/zoom):

900px, 90%:   old 900 - 133.3/0.9 - 8 = 758.7   honest 1000 - 133.3 - 8 = 858.7   ->  100px short
900px, 110%:  old 900 - 133.3/1.1 - 8 = 770.8   honest  818.2 - 133.3 - 8 = 676.9 ->  93.9px too tall

(Worked on the band gate's own synthetic console at top = 133.3, so both lines are arithmetic on the same top. On the live float the top itself moves with the zoom, which is why the matrix's deltas are larger.) The second line is the overshoot: a room larger than the viewport can hold lets the box draw past the bottom, and release then clamps it back - the jump the reader sees at 110%. The first line is the early stop: at 90% the room is a tenth of the viewport short, and no amount of dragging reaches the edge.

And nothing anywhere subtracted the pane. A console inside the assistant pane's scrollport could grow behind the reply box and past the pane's own bottom by 17.5px at 100%, before any zoom error.

4. The fix: one band, one unit, one floor, one clamp

public/core/console-grip.js was created from the grip that used to sit in public/app.js (406 lines moved, the module is 408 including its new band), and the page loads it before app.js. The band is one function, and it computes both rooms in layout pixels:

paneRoom = (paneBottom - top - 8)          // null when there is no scrollport
viewRoom = (innerHeight / z - top - 8)     // layout, not visual
room     = min(paneRoom ?? Infinity, viewRoom)
edge     = room === paneRoom ? "pane" : "viewport"

public/core/console-grip.js:72 band(), :82 the pane room, :83 the viewport room, :219 the edge recorded on the drag. :96 limit() is the one clamp; the move (:268) and the release (:334) both call it, so there is one place that can stop the box and it stops at the band.

The press reads the pane bottom from the nearest .job-body (public/core/console-grip.js:194), and the floor is the box's own computed min-height in layout px. The release no longer clears the inline height: clearing it left the box sized by the last frame, so the first frame after the release drew the whole travel (the jump the reader described as the "bounce"). It now clamps and writes the height it reached.

The direction the reader described

The grip sits on the box's bottom edge, so a drag upward makes the box shorter; its only stop is the floor. The drag that expands is the one that goes down, and its stop is the pane or the viewport edge. The band gate asserts both: the growing drag stops on the named edge minus the 8px gap, the shrinking drag stops on the floor, and in neither does the corner leave the hand. This is stated here rather than glossed, because "the stop is only ever the pane or viewport edge" is a statement about the growing drag.

6. The extraction, and the gates that read it

  • public/core/console-grip.js: press, move, end (release), step, band, limit and the edge watch. Published as globalThis.wtConsoleGrip, with band, limit, capOf, release and the read-only wtConsoleGripDrag record for gates.
  • public/app.js: the grip's 406 lines removed, 31 lines of wiring left (:10640 wtConsoleGrip.install({...}) with the page's store, view and scroll hooks). No band, clamp or gap arithmetic remains in the page.
  • public/index.html: one classic script before app.js.
  • tools/verify-console-corner-drag.mjs: the room and the release clamp now come from GRIP.band(...) and GRIP.release(...) (:80, :778), not from a local copy of the formula. This is the gate whose cases span 2 widths, 2 pane sizes and 2 edge shapes: it was the one that had been re-deriving the band and so could not see the unit error.
  • tools/verify-console-resize-release.mjs: the release's room comes from window.wtConsoleGrip.band(...) (:160), and the release is driven through the module's own pointer handling. 8 of 8.
  • tools/verify-console-grip-band.mjs (new): one band, both edges, three zooms, two window heights, grow and shrink: 26 checks. It reads the band from the module the page serves and never re-derives a room.
  • tools/verify-console-height.mjs: the drag presses the grip (the console's own corner stopped being the gesture's target when the grip became its own element) and the fixture is centred so the viewport room the band measures is inside it: 60 of 60. Before this cut the same gate pressed the old native corner and expected 420px inside a 320px room: 30 of 59 checks failed.
  • tools/verify-console-resize.mjs: the up leg's floor now comes from wtConsoleGripDrag.min, the module's own number, instead of "every step is 20px forever". The floor is 34px and the last step lands on it: 41 of 41.

The two gates that opened the deleted public/style.css now read the sheets public/index.html loads in order, and the fonts gate's reasoning-row selector follows the class the one-console-stream cut left (.jl-think).

One clamp. Before, five places in app.js computed or applied a band: the press's room (:10829), the floor read (:10724), outGripLimit (:10812), outGripRelease (:10998) and the move's clamp (:10923). After, there is one band() and one limit() in the module, and the move and the release are its only callers.
