6. One two-stage chain whose second stage waits, through the real routes

A wait is what makes a chain hold, so the end-to-end check is not a plain chain: the first definition climbs into a second whose last stage ends only on the reviewer's answer. hold-local (kind local, one stage work, next: "hold-review") and hold-review (kind investigation, two stages: draft ends on branch-exists, answer ends on pr-comments). The panel was the real server.js, booted against a throwaway config by tools/panel-testkit.mjs; the definitions were written by the route, not by hand into the file. 

PUT  two-definition chain accepted  [status 200]
PUT  no definition dropped: a review exit and a {{branch}} prompt are valid  [dropped []]
GET  catalogue read  [status 200]
GET  both definitions are in the merged catalogue  [hold-local, hold-review]
GET  the chain link survived the save and the read  [hold-local.next = hold-review]
GET  the second definition carries its two stages  [draft -> answer]
GET  the waiting stage kept its reviewer exit  [{"check":"pr-comments"}]
GET  the closed vocabularies still travel  [local,investigation,turnover]
DISK the live file holds the waiting stage and its exit  [{"check":"pr-comments"}]
WAIT reviewWaitFor registered a wait from the read-back stage  [answer / pr-comments]
WAIT the wait carries its document and its thread  [hold-review / thread-row-514]
WAIT its first interval is the one-hour floor  [3600000]
WAIT it reads as the reviewer's kind, not a run hold
WAIT not due at the cut, due one interval later
WAIT the first claim is granted
WAIT a comment newer than lastSeen matches the exit  [a comment arrived at 2026-11-28T12:01:00.000Z, after 2026-11-28T11:00:00.000Z]
WAIT the settle advances the stage and closes the wait
WAIT the Bitbucket payload advances the same exit  [a comment arrived at 2026-11-28T12:02:00.000Z, after 2026-11-28T12:00:00.000Z]
CHAIN-WAIT PASSED: the chain holds on a stage that waits

Steps 1-9 prove the save and the read: the chain link, both definitions, the two stages and the reviewer exit come back from the route's own merged catalogue, and the same exit is in the live file on disk. Steps 10-15 are the moved code run against the definition the ROUTE returned: reviewWaitFor registers the pr-comments wait for answer, with its document, thread and one-hour first interval; waitKind reads it as the reviewer's; claimWait grants the first claim. Steps 16-18 drive the read and the settle: a comment newer than lastSeen matches the exit, the settle advances the stage and closes the wait, and a Bitbucket-shaped payload (epoch-millisecond stamps, the real reviewer shape) does the same through the second normaliser. What this does not run is a live run's wake on a thread: that is the sweep, and test/pipeline-wait.test.js drives it with a fake job store.
