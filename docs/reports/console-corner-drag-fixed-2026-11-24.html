1. The cause, as the two reports left it

Row 316 was briefed from two readings that agree, so nothing here is re-derived:

  • docs/reports/console-corner-drag-measured-2026-11-24.html - 32 drags on the live panel, 25 failing, 24 of them on the grip's bottom edge: the size was written correctly and the corner did not move.
  • docs/reports/console-corner-drag-code-2026-11-24.html - six defects ranked, one primary: the height has one writer but the box's POSITION has three. At the foot, scrollTop's maximum is a function of the console's height, so the scrollport is a second writer of the top edge the press froze (public/app.js:10623), and the release's own foot write (public/app.js:10722) is the third.

The physics is two lines, and the measurement below is what they look like:

max       = scrollHeight - clientHeight, and scrollHeight includes the console's height
at the foot: scrollTop = max, so the box's top edge on screen is V - height - K

grow  by N: max grows by N, nothing clamps, the top stays, the corner follows the pointer
shrink by N: max shrinks by N, scrollTop is now past it, the browser's clamp moves the content DOWN by N,
              the top edge goes down with it and the box's bottom does not move at all
release:      setScroll(body, scrollHeight) moves the top edge back up by the drag's own travel

2. What changed

2.1 The gesture owns the offset it freezes

The press now records the transcript's scrollTop beside the top edge and the grab (public/app.js:10644 to 10668), stands the browser's scroll anchor down on the transcript it is dragging (public/app.js:10671), and every move writes that offset back (public/app.js:10727) after the height. Three things follow:

  • a writer that moves the offset during the drag (the anchor, a pin, a poll) is undone in the same task, so the top edge really is a constant and not a function of anything else that scrolls;
  • a drag that dips below the transcript's floor and comes back re-asserts the offset the moment the content has room for it again, so the corner returns to the hand. Measured in the gate's crossing shape: 36px lost at 1600 and 100px at 820 without this write, 0 with it;
  • the anchor had to go. It is the loop that holds the reader's place when a row lands above, and the old design kept it on for exactly that reason (the note at public/board/console.js:3656 to 3662). Measured, it is also the writer that cancels the reader's drag: the same slow drag at 1600 wrote the height 60 and moved the corner 0, and at 820 followed perfectly, because at 1600 the anchor had picked a node below the box. Standing it down for the length of the drag wins the case that happens every time over the one that happens when a row lands mid-drag.

A row that lands ABOVE the box during a drag is not the anchor's job any more: the frame after each move reads the box once and scrolls the transcript by exactly the drift it measures (public/app.js:10735), which the growth itself made room for. Measured: a state poll landing under the hand moved the top edge 24.3px, and this is what brings it back without re-deriving the height (the size stays the pointer's travel, not the drift's).

2.2 The release stops undoing the drag

The release clamped, stored, and then wrote the foot - a scroll of exactly the drag's travel, in the same task as the height. It now records where the drag left the transcript instead (noteCardBodyScroll, public/app.js:10801 to 10808): the transcript is that far from its foot, it stops following until the reader is back on it, and following resumes on their own scroll. That is the visible behaviour change this fix carries, and it is the one the code report's Option 1 named: a resize the reader asked for takes the stream's follow away with it.

2.4 The floor, and the slack turn

A drag stopped at 1px and the box drew 18px: the console's padding box (8px top and bottom plus its two borders) is the smallest height it can draw, so the store kept a number the box never was. The floor is now the larger of the sheet's min-height and that padding box (public/app.js:10639), the move stops on it, and the release stores it.

The second half is the slack turn. .turn.is-slack's flex: 1 1 auto grew the console into whatever room the pane had left, so a console the reader had given 20px drew 105.41px - the number the gesture wrote, the store kept and the next restore put back was not the box on screen, and a drag to the floor stopped 105px above it. A console with an explicit height is now flex: 0 0 auto (public/style.css:6475), and the room a pane has left goes back to the spacer above the turns, where the sheet already puts it for a short conversation.

3. The gate

The gate is the acceptance instrument, and it needed one thing to be able to answer at all: the gesture lives in the page, so the panel serving it has to be the build under test. Restarting the reader's panel under them is not something a gate may do, and a second instance has no transcript to drag (cards are per process). --serve <checkout> answers the page's own requests from that checkout's public/ and lets everything else - the API, the SSE stream, the row scans - come from the running panel. One gate, one set of live data, two trees.

Three checks were added, each aimed at a defect the old gate could not see:

  • the corner after the release (tools/verify-console-corner-drag.mjs:657). The old gate read the corner at every step and never after the pointerup, which is where the whole travel was being undone. It is judged only when the release kept the number the pointer asked for, and only at or above the transcript's floor.
  • the transcript's floor (line 588). The console's height is inside the transcript's scrollHeight, so a transcript on its foot has no offset room for a shrink: the clamp takes the top edge down with it, and the box stops following. That is a floor like the sheet's min-height, judged by the size and store checks, not as a corner the gesture lost. The floor is read where the press found it - how far the transcript was from its foot.
  • the node and the card's signature are read DURING the drag (line 575). A deferred rebuild landing in the pointerup is the release's business; the gate's question is whether the node under the hand survived the travel. Read after the release, it could not tell the two apart.

4. The up-drag, and the two mechanisms that were measured and refused

One part of the brief's sentence - "the travel the pointer makes is the travel the edge makes" - is not true below the height the press found, and this section is why. The transcript is at its foot: the console's bottom edge IS the end of its content. Moving that edge up on screen means the content's end must rise above the viewport's bottom, and the scrollport cannot scroll past its content. Room has to come from somewhere, and both places were built and measured:

the mechanismwhat it measuredwhy it was refused
a pad at the end of the transcript, so its content height stands still while the box shrinks a 120px pad lifted the composer from the pane's bottom (875) to 755; a 300px pad to 575. The reader's composer floats in the middle of the pane with blank under it it trades a corner that does not follow for a composer that is not where the reader left it
the same room, inside the console's own wrap (a margin under the box) the corner follows for the whole drag, and after a 400px shrink the console's grip sat at 265 while the transcript's viewport starts at 303.8: the next press could not reach the grip at all (caught by the gate, at 460) a corner that can walk out of the transcript it lives in is worse than one that stops at its floor

So the corner follows every pixel the transcript has room for, and stops at the transcript's floor with the box on the console's own 18px floor. The alternative - the console's bottom edge being a different element from the transcript's end - is the code report's Option 4: a layout of its own for the two panes with the most follow and fold behaviour in the page, and not a landing for this row. What the reader gets in the meantime is written down in section 5.

6. The followers the gate did not prove

The code report ranked six defects. This landing fixes the primary one, the four followers the gate could see, and the floor; four remain, and none of them is visible to a drag:

  • a restore that skips the release's clamp (report 4.2) - applyStoredHeight applies a stored number with no floor and lifts the sheet's cap to match it. The gate's store check reads a height restored by the fixture's own setOutHeight, so it cannot see this.
  • a cap destroyed by one over-cap drag (report 4.3, second half) - after a drag past the cap the inline max-height: none makes parseFloat of the computed value NaN, so no later press can read the sheet's cap back. The gate drags once per case; a two-drag case would be the test, and it is not in this row's matrix.
  • an ungated fold flush under the pointer (report 4.4) - the ResizeObserver-driven command-card fold mark rewrites text nodes inside the console. The gate counts childList mutations inside the console and measured 0 in every case, so there is nothing to prove here yet.
  • a restore before the layout that makes it valid (report 4.7) - latent, no caller hits it today.

They are named here rather than quietly fixed because each one wants its own test, and the brief was explicit: only the ranked followers the gate proves.
