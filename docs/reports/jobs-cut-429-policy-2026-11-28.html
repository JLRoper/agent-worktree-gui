1. The cut, and why this one first

lib/jobs.js is a run state machine with an HTTP client grown through it. The plan at docs/arch-review-r1-a.md:366-394 names four seams in it (J1 gateway transport, J2 persistence, J3 artifacts and the worktree, J4 steering and stopping). J1 is the biggest, and its first half is the one cluster in the file with no this in it at all: the 429 backpressure policy.

Five names move: RETRY_429_DEFAULTS, retry429Settings, retryAfterMs, retry429DelayMs and fmtWait. They read the panel's config and a response's Retry-After header and write nothing. They were already exported, and tools/verify-429-hold.mjs already drove them through the shipped Jobs, so a move has a gate that predates it. That is why it is the smallest cut that leaves every caller working: no method leaves the class, no field moves, no call site changes.

What stays, and has to stay for now: the hold that spends them. The POST loop inside create (lib/jobs.js:4917-5006) owns the attempt count, the waiting status, the cancel that beats the retry and the failure sentence a spent budget writes. That is state, and it is the other half of J1.

2. Why the other four cuts need their own rows

Each remaining cut moves a method out of the Jobs class, so each one has to decide what the class keeps as a delegating wrapper and what state travels with the move. None of that is in this row's scope, and the plan says why:

4J3 artifacts and worktreemeasureTree, repointPath, sweepArtifactsThey mutate job records (the artifact list, the repointed path), so the seam is a record contract, not a pure move.
5J4 steering and stoppingapprove, steer, answerQuestion, controlContextThree control verbs over one status field, and the vocabulary is shared with public/core/steerable.js.

The split of J1 into two rows is deliberate. A row that moved the policy AND rewrote create's POST would have one commit nobody can review as a pure move, and the pure-move property is the only thing that makes a cut this size safe at all.

The reds are named so they can be judged. Three of the four post-cut runs were on a box other agents were also building on, and both failures are timing assertions:

  • test/job-settle-state-agreement.test.js:121, "the primed board read carries the live status" (actual undefined, expected running). Red in 3 of 4 loaded runs after the cut. It is red at the turnover head too: run standalone against the exported a7ac20b1 tree it failed on attempt 3 of 6 with the identical assertion, and it is 3 of 3 green standalone in this tree. The mechanism is in the panel, not in this cut: /api/state answers the last built frame while a rebuild runs behind it (server.js:918-928 and the comment at server.js:944-950 describing exactly this "state contradicts jobs" window), and the test reads /api/state immediately after /api/jobs goes live. Under load the rebuild is slower than the assertion's patience.
  • test/job-store-atomic.test.js, "a reader sees the whole old file or the whole new one, never a fragment". Red once, in the most loaded run. Standalone it is 5 of 5 green on the head tree and 5 of 5 green here, and this cut touches no write path.
  • lib/jobs.test.js is a panel-driving harness, not a node:test file, and it is red at the head as well: 56 pass / 3 fail here, and the same three FAIL lines at the turnover head (its phase 4 and 5 assertions race the stub's POST counter). It is quoted for completeness and is not the gate this cut is judged on.

The honest reading: on 3 of 4 loaded runs this tree is red on tests that a clean a7ac20b1 passes. I did not tune the tests and I did not move on from it until I could reproduce the same assertion at the head, which is what section 5 shows. The cut itself has no timing surface: it adds one import and moves five pure functions.
