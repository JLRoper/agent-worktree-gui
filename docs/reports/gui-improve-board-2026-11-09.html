1. The pipeline, and what this round was asked to prove

gui-improve is a review pipeline for one visual surface at a time. Five stages, three roles, three gates, six handoffs. The roles matter more than the stage names: only the implementer can change the product, only the verifier can measure it, and only the reviewer can pass or fail it. A stage is a session with one job and a tree (or, in the single-tree model, one commit); the panel cuts the tree, settles the run, and writes the record.

That is the loop's defect, and it is structural rather than personal. G3 says an acceptance leaves no note open; it does not say a note has to be about something a stage can change. A note about pipelineRun is a true defect of the run, meets the gate's shape (surface, defect, evidence, fix), and is impossible for correct to close. The gate therefore has no terminating condition once such a note is written: correct cannot fix it, compare cannot fix it, review cannot accept while it is open, and the round reset is capped at 3. The row sat 4h 06m 30s after the interrupted correct and was closed by the reader at 19:07:01 with "Decided: land, not re-run."

What the second review could not do

It could not fail the change, and it said so: "the change-set needs no edit, and the correct stage should not re-open it." It also could not pass the round, because the record was wrong in two places the panel owns. A gate that can only reject on a defect the pipeline cannot repair is a gate that hands every such round to a person, which is what happened here, after 4h 06m 30s of waiting.

Two caveats keep this comparison honest. Judgement

The per-stage model is not proven. In this round it failed all three of its own acceptance clauses.

(1) Every stage from one frozen parent: false. Compare and review were cut from main's tip at 99f2bd77 and correct from 7611c37b, while the round sha is f0b80c7. (2) Every stage tree attached in the chart: false for implement, which has no trees key and no tree of its own. (3) The implementation committed on its stage tree: false in form; the commit exists on a reused draft tree, and the record names a branch that does not contain it.

What it bought that the single tree did not: nothing measurable here. What it cost: four extra trees to cut and clean, one reused tree, an assembly that refused, a hand-built landing, a relocation PR, two record defects no stage could fix, and a duplicated implement entry. The control needed none of that, and its one line of commits is the whole walk.

The honest recommendation is not "trees are bad". It is that the model's benefit is entirely in what the panel writes about the trees, and in this round the panel wrote less than the model needs: the round parent, the implement tree and the branch field were all missing or wrong. Until those are written at cut time, the per-stage model is extra machinery with a demonstrated failure mode and no demonstrated gain, and a round should use one tree.

8. What this page does not claim

This page is an argument from one round. It does not claim:

  • That the pipeline is wrong for every GUI change. It is one round, one surface, two reviews.
  • That the change is good design. It measures as a real, confined improvement and the record says so; taste is the reader's.
  • That the 14 px growth is acceptable in general. It was accepted here because the card's height did not move and the panel still opens at 340x520; no height budget exists in G2, and the proof run's first attempt grew a panel by 20 px and drew a reject.
  • That the reviewer was wrong to reject. The two open notes are real defects of the record; the point is that no stage could close them.
  • That #256 landed itself. Its change and ledger reached main later, at 7bc1cb56, by a later landing.
  • That the token cost is known. It is not recoverable from this machine's state, and the page says so where it matters.
  • That the stage-entry sum is "time per stage". Two stage ids carry two entries each and the record has no attempt field, so the sum is over entries, not stages.
  • That the row's startedAt is the start. It is 14:57:08.971Z, a done-time backfill; the real clock is createdAt to doneAt.
